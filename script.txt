ADITYA: Slides 7–8 (about 3 minutes)
Slide 7: Our Solution
Thank you, Jishita.
Let me start with something we all use. A weather app tells you whether to carry an umbrella before you leave home. RakshaRoute tells you which road is safe before you take it.
(Point to the map.) For Priya's trip tonight, the fastest route gets a Safety Score of 41 out of 100. The safest route scores 89. And it's only three minutes longer.
Here's how we get there, in five steps. (Point to each step.)
One: Collect. We gather data on street lighting, how busy an area is, public incident data and reports from our community.
Two: Score. Every street gets a live Safety Score from 0 to 100, and that score changes with the time of day.
Three: Route. The app suggests the safest path, usually with only a few extra minutes of travel.
Four: Watch. During the trip, it notices if something unusual happens, like an unexpected stop or a detour.
Five: Alert. If something looks wrong, it first checks with her. Then, if needed, it alerts her trusted contacts, and 112 is always one tap away.
Because, as we believe, every woman deserves to get home, not just get there fast.
Slide 8: Under the Hood
Now let me show you the serious AI behind this simple map.
(Point to the hexagon map.) We divide the city into thousands of small hexagonal cells, using a system called the H3 grid. Each cell gets a Safety Score that updates every hour. Violet cells are safer; pink cells score lower.
(Point to each tile, slowly and confidently.)
Geospatial risk scoring. Each cell is scored using lighting, activity and incident data.
Graph-based route optimisation. We treat the city's roads as a weighted graph, where every road has a time and a safety cost. Then we find a safety-weighted shortest path: the best balance of speed and safety.
Time-aware prediction. The same street can be busy and bright at seven PM, and empty at eleven. So its score changes through the night.
Movement anomaly detection. The system learns what a normal trip looks like, and flags sudden stops, detours or unusual speed changes.
Crowdsourced data validation. Every user report gets a trust score, so spam and false reports are filtered out before they affect anyone's route.
Privacy-first architecture. Location is shared only during an active trip, it's encrypted, and it's deleted afterwards.
So it's serious AI, behind a map anyone can use. Now let's see what Priya actually sees on her phone. Varma.

Slide 10: Trust by Design
Now, an important question: would you hand your location to just any app?
A safety app must first be safe itself. Women will only use RakshaRoute if they trust it completely. So we built trust in from day one, with four principles. (Point to each card.)
One: consent first. Tracking happens only during a trip she starts. It's off by default.
Two: data minimised. Trip data is encrypted and automatically deleted after 24 hours.
Three: fair scoring. Our scores describe conditions, like lighting, activity and time of day. They never label communities. And any score can be explained.
Four: human-checked reports. Reports from the community are verified before they can influence anyone's route.
(Point to the shield.) Her location. Her control. We've thought about ethics, not just features.
